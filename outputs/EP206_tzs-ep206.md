## Key Facts

> **F1** The 5–10 year claim: deposits will not keep the household as rates fall. Equities and insurance are the receiving pipes. US 1980s is the named rhyme — slower growth, older savers, more need for a return. Wang wrote because he could find single-essay books (green, digital) but not one file on all five plus the property/LGFV side. Chapters 1–5 are the essays; 6 is real-estate and local-government debt as infrastructure finance that built the roads you still walk, not only a bad residual.

> **F2** Stock of the old circuit: 50–60% of credit still sketched as bank loans in his 20-year cartoon; another line on the tape puts 60–70% on that channel. UK banks at ≈4% corporate loans is the contrast. That is why ICBC-class balance sheets are the starting ticker and why Wells Fargo (Buffett letter, retail-bank case) is the named US compare, not a growth multiple. Household and personal loans look weak — 'too much money, too few projects.'

> **F3** Cheap risk assets: HS300 low; old-light Moutai/Eastroc/Midea with tiny public-fund holdings; AI and optics 'confused' and expensive. A 10% grower in a calm compounder is the thought experiment next to A500 vol. Digital finance is one of the five essays. Diligence the deposit-to-equity pipe, not a one-year bank NIM. Public-fund AUM grew fast while old-light holdings stayed tiny — that is the empty-fund print next to Moutai at 16–20x. Wang names smaller excellent-student banks (Taizhou-class) as well as the big SOE book. Insurance may take the first mark-to-market hit; banks still hold the loan stock. Wells Fargo is the named US retail-bank letter on the tape. The 5–10 year claim is the household leaving a 1% deposit for equities, insurance, and funds.
