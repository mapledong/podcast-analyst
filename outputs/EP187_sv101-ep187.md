## Key Facts

> **F1** Mechanics and scale: ≈500 students inverted pyramid (≈250 high school, 100+ middle, <100 primary); no fixed rooms, schedules, or rankings; guardians/learners titles, mentors, life coaches, four houses (Chizi/Fupu/Shangshan/Wuji). ≈100 courses including 21 APs, but focus is competencies — critical thinking, information literacy, creation, collaboration; Life Skill classes cover outdoor survival, cooking, first aid, and life discussions. Tuition 200,000+ RMB/year; outcomes split — Harvard/MIT/liberal arts paths alongside direct startups or niche tracks like insect art.

> **F2** 'One Small Step' and super high schoolers: 1+X structure — year one rebuilds learning habits; X stage 1–3 years at student pace; three exit paths are funded independent startups, stable employment income, or commercialized project transfer. Cases: Xu Haoming's smart wake-up lamp, 15-year-old Liu Yizhou's design studio, Jiang Yin's dyslexia-reading aid company (60-person team via social recruiting, 8 on core project). M48 hackathon is national, airfare/lodging covered, AI-for-Good themes; Chen Guangyu debuted at the first edition before Moonshot paper and Musk like. VCs and industry leaders judge 48-hour builds on-site.

> **F3** Ten-year experiment risks: 2017 launched on training licenses for non-compulsory education; 2019 campus build stalled in pandemic — hotel openings (three sites in 2020); 'double reduction' made full-time model non-compliant, forcing move to the tennis center. Wang reflects on early arrogance — mission to 'sustain civilization' without self-cultivation first; rejected private-school mergers fearing economic-cycle dilution; personal debt and home collateral. Conclusion: not a universal answer — suits highly self-driven, disciplined students; ≈500 is the right size; ideal is sharing the model, not factory-scale replication.

## Mental Model · *Competency-First × Third Path × Simulator Upgrade*

> **Components**
>
> Domain knowledge answers more questions; competencies (judgment, aesthetics, critique, collaboration) decide which questions matter. Diplomas offer low-cost consensus and platform resource access; 'One Small Step' tries to replace that with real work products. Schools simulate society's next stage — factories in the industrial era, project-based creation and lifelong learning in the AI era.
>
> **Application**
>
> Underwrite innovative education: Private:Tanyue School is a high-cost personalization bet (Long/Low) — moat is trust and graduate work chains, not scale. Private:Moonshot indirectly signals super-teen talent via hackathon pipeline. PLTR (Long/Low) and peers scouting younger talent is a new AI hiring frontier, but school models don't map linearly to public revenue; watch competency credentials and portfolio rails (GitHub-like) displacing diplomas.

## Key Insights

> **1.** College is no longer the only exit.
>
> **Q** What does 'One Small Step' bet on?
>
> **A** AI lowers startup barriers — 48-hour hackathons validate value creation; three graduation paths require real social outcomes. Interviewed teens are fifty-fifty or skip 'conventional jobs,' seeing university as optional for skills and networks they need now.

> **2.** Teach competencies, not knowledge piles.
>
> **Q** What to learn in the AI era?
>
> **A** Judgment, aesthetics, critical thinking — AI floods information; filtering and verification matter. Competencies transfer across domains (analysis in finance vs biotech); habits of mind resist AI distillation better than exam memorization.

> **3.** Innovative schools must hand-craft the first chip.
>
> **Q** Why not mainstream?
>
> **A** Personalization + mentors + project courses = high cost; regulatory uncertainty, moves, and debt nearly killed the school. Wang: deeply practice educational essence and hand-craft chip one before the factory; ≈500 students is the responsible ceiling for families and mission.
