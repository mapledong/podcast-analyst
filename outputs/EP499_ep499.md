## Background

> EP499, Nov 6, about 46 minutes. Naimi founded Abstract and has been early-stage investing since 2016. Patrick wants the state of venture: goalposts, revenue quality, and why LPs are asking harder questions. He says every Abstract fund was raised too small. He could have kept the same return profile with about twice the capital.

---

## Key Facts

> **F1** Goalposts and messy growth. In 2016 the best case people underwrote was $10 billion. Then $100 billion. Now a trillion. He counts three already: SpaceX, Anthropic, OpenAI. His bet is two or three more, not forty. Companies growing 10, 15, or 20 times are not clean 90% gross-margin SaaS. The revenue is low or negative margin, concentrated, annualized off last month because they sell data, consumer rather than enterprise, or usage rather than a contract. Data labeling can grow fast at 15-20% margins because the data is expensive. Products that sit on a foundation model hand the margin to the lab. He still sees margins inflecting: portfolio companies growing 200-700% this year while margins move from negative 15% to positive 35%. Vertical software at 100 times revenue is being compared with public software near 13 times. The bridge he accepts is labor replacement. A law firm spends little on software and a lot on paralegals. Capture 20% of the paralegal budget and the company is no longer a small software TAM. In 2021, $8 million of SaaS revenue could raise at a $1 billion valuation on 3-5 times growth and a seven-year path to $100 million ARR. He would not get out of bed for that book now.

> **F2** How rounds clear. In 2021 a loser of a round came back a couple of weeks later at 2 times. Now the loser does not want to wait. The parallel secondary, which he says everyone has been on the wrong side of at least once, is usually 3, 4, or 5 times, not 2, alongside the primary. He does not read that as a sick market. The companies are capital intensive and do not want the whole check in the primary. Offers also get pulled or rewritten, which does remind him of 2021. Founders who are not posting those growth rates should compare themselves with their own revenue style, not with the absolute top-line prints.

> **F3** Funds and LPs. A venture fund is built for an 18-24 month initial deployment, plus reserves. In the last 18-24 months the mega platforms have raised two or three times, so deployment is about 3 times faster than the model. They underestimated how many companies they would want, the price, and the dollars required for the ownership they modeled. They come back sooner and larger. The LP choice is fewer shots with the ownership intact, or the same shots with less ownership and a capped upside. LPs also expected more liquidity. A 35-50% venture weight is often 30-50% of AUM in three or four companies: SpaceX, Anthropic, OpenAI, Databricks. Those names have to list or sell before the asset-class weight comes down. An LP who missed SpaceX has not gotten that liquidity. Abstract's own mistake, he says, is raising every fund too small. Twice the capital would have kept the return profile, let them lead more often, and given founders more money.

## Mental Model · *Trillion Goalposts × Messy Revenue × 3x Deployment*

> **Components**
>
> The dream outcome went from $10 billion to $100 billion to $1 trillion. Three names are already there. He will not underwrite forty. Growth of 10-20 times is not 90% margin contracted SaaS. Labeling margins are 15-20%. Some books grow 200-700% and lift margins from negative 15% to positive 35%. A 100 times private multiple versus 13 times public software is a labor-budget argument, not a software-budget argument. Secondaries clear at 3-5 times beside the primary because the companies need the capital. Mega funds deploy about 3 times faster than an 18-24 month plan. LP concentration is three or four companies, and liquidity has to come from those companies.
>
> **Application**
>
> Private:SpaceX, Private:Anthropic, and Private:OpenAI are the liquidity names that actually move LP weights, along with Databricks. Do not mark a public software multiple of 13 times as the comp for a labor-replacement book. Abstract's lesson is fund size: too small, not too large. A founder who is not growing 10 times should compare within a revenue style.

>
> **Q** What is hiding in the revenue?
