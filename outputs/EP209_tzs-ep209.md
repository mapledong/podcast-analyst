## Key Facts

> **F1** The observer. Fan started in 2002 as a securities and fund reporter and stayed five years. He then spent nine years at a mutual-fund company and left at the end of 2015 to start Smart Investor, a content shop with a public account, an app, and a site. In 2018, the twentieth year of the formal public-fund industry, the securities association asked for a book. He interviewed about 70 to 80 people: regulators, founding partners, famous public managers, and people who had already gone private. He calls that book a stereo history of policy, behavior, and the market. The Capital Group translation is the foreign file next to that domestic reporting. He had already followed the firm for more than twenty years. A colleague in 2015 told him the growth complex was about $1.5 trillion, and that the firm founded in 1931 had been only about $20 million for nearly twenty years. Ninety years is the point of the book. The details are a textbook because the market changes and the management problem does not.

> **F2** Own money versus other people's money. A professional with his own capital and no leverage can actually be long-term. Duan Yongping playing golf is hard to copy. Duan can hold Moutai as a substitute for bank interest. An institution that needs 10-15%, sometimes said as 20%, on the whole portfolio cannot use that asset in that role. The manager is dancing in shackles. The client may not accept three or four rough years on the way to the target. So the institution has to underwrite the liability, the holding period, and the path, not only the asset. A personal account that puts 5% into something Duan would size as a core holding is not the same decision. Pop Mart is the same lesson: personal money can hold a view the fund's clients will not fund through the drawdown.

> **F3** Why one manager is not an institution. A polite senior who lets everyone talk and then splits the difference produced mediocre results, because nobody really believed the compromise. A single-manager shop failed the other way. Around Sputnik, the growth portfolio, Hall on the tape, went to cash. Lovelace stayed with Eisenhower and stayed in stocks, so the growth fund became the conservative one and the conservative fund became the aggressive one. One person will be wrong across a long career. A firm that wants to last cannot bet that one person stays right. The third path was multiple managers. The idea was Jon Lovelace, the founder's son and the second-generation head, not the founder. In the 1970s they faced another client-first test. Peers launched money-market funds. By 1974 the stock market was already crushed, and a sales team would have loved a cash product. Jon said pushing cash was wrong. If they were active stock investors, they should be selling stocks. That is the institutional tell Fan wants: the product follows the belief, not the easy channel, and no single temperament owns the whole book.

## Mental Model · *Liability First × Multiple Managers × Ninety Years*

> **Components**
>
> Personal capital can be a deposit substitute. Institutional capital has a 10-15% portfolio target and clients who will not sit through three or four bad years. A committee that only compromises is mediocre. One manager will invert his own style in a panic, as the growth book did at Sputnik. Multiple managers, Jon Lovelace's design, stop one error from being the firm. In 1973-74 they would not hide in money-market funds. The firm itself was about $20 million for twenty years after 1931 and about $1.5 trillion in the growth complex by 2015. Fan followed them for more than twenty years before the translation. That gap is the tell. Time is the asset of the institution, not a star year.
>
> **Application**
>
> Private:Capital Group is the case, not a tradable stock. 600519.SZ is the deposit-substitute holding an institution with a 10-15% target should not pretend to copy. 9992.HK is the personal-money position the same way. Judge a firm by whether one temperament can sink the book, and by what it sold in 1974, not by whether the manager looks busy.

## Key Insights

> **1.** Twenty years at $20 million is the start of the story.
>
> **Q** What was Capital Group in 1931?
>
> **A** Founded that year, still about $20 million two decades later. The growth complex he was shown in 2015 was about $1.5 trillion.

> **2.** Other people's money cannot copy a personal account.
>
> **Q** Why is Duan's Moutai hard to learn?
>
> **A** For him it replaces bank interest. A fund that needs 10-15% on the portfolio, with clients who hate a three or four year drawdown, is dancing in shackles.

> **3.** One manager will be wrong. The firm should not be.
>
> **Q** What did Sputnik teach them?
>
> **A** The growth book went to cash and the conservative book stayed in. Jon Lovelace, the son, built multiple managers so one panic is not the firm. In 1974 they still sold stocks, not money funds.
