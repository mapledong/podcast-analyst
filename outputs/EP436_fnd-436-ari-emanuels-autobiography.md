## Key Facts

> **F1** Rage as OS: every story starts and ends in rage because rage clarifies. Keep calm and carry on is the enemy. Rage to make them hear you, keep them honest, or you are them. Franklin: there is no little enemy — the tattoo he would put on his knuckles. He is a Hollywood agent and, by the numbers, damn good (he says, by the numbers); rage is the secret sauce. Humans want; dissatisfaction is how the species dominated the planet. Frustration to anxiety to rage to action. Rage means shit is finally going to get done.

> **F2** Mail room and volume: he is about 26 and too old for the mail room. They tell him to get out. He does not. A mentor eventually opens a door. MO is not cleverness — it is 10 calls a day for days until the person takes the meeting. Friends ask if he is always like this; the book says yes. He will not stay an assistant. The occupational hazard is the job: if you cannot rage, you cannot agent.

> **F3** Founder arc: Senra reads him as more founder than agent by the time of the tape — WME/Endeavor after the 1995 break from CAA culture, then UFC/live sports. The memoir is entertaining because it is unembarrassed about the heat. Live Nation and the sports-betting complex sit next to the talent shop. The method does not change when the P&L gets bigger: more asks, more enemies, more action. Ten calls a day is still the unit.

## Mental Model · *Rage First × No Little Enemy × 10 Calls a Day*

> **Components**
>
> Treat calm as the competitor's slogan. Clarify the problem with heat. Volume of asks beats a perfect script. Too old is not a no. Do not die as an assistant. Dissatisfaction is the engine, not a mood to medicate. When the shop becomes a conglomerate, keep the same occupational hazard. Keep the method boring enough to repeat every week. Numbers in the room beat stories in the hallway. The founder who will not walk the floor, make the tenth call, or rewrite the Saturday software is already the cartel.
>
> **Application**
>
> LYV is the live-events cousin of the Ari machine — tickets, buildings, heat. DKNG is the sports-wagering layer that now sits on the same live inventory. DIS is the named studio/talent foil an agent either feeds or fights. Fade polite-agency decks. Size any talent business that will not make the 10th call.
