## Background

> Patrick reunites with Etched co-founders Gavin Ubieto and Rob Lockett three years after most experts told him twenty-something founders could not beat NVIDIA-class incumbents. Etched builds Sohu — a transformer-native inference ASIC delivered as a complete rack (chip, boards, power, interconnects, manufacturing) designed after ChatGPT rewrote the workload.
>
> Rob's path runs through stage-four bone cancer in high school and a GPT-4 vision moment that diagnosed his old tumor photo in seconds — then hit a paywall, crystallizing the infrastructure gap. Gavin came from kernel optimization (Ex-Nor, acquired by Apple) and saw that data movement, not math, dominates decode speed. Together they argue inference COGS will define software economics and that general-purpose GPU stacks carry decades of buffer built for IoT-era generality that no longer applies.

---

## Key Facts

> **F1** First-tapeout milestone and demand: Etched became the first post-ChatGPT hardware startup to tape out a working chip on the first attempt, with $800M raised and more than $1B of customer demand for Sohu. Product is a full inference rack — not a chip alone — vertically integrating cold plates, interconnects, Taiwan factory test stations, and a two-megawatt in-office data center for 24/7 hardware cycles.

> **F2** Technical thesis — transformer-native + low voltage: Architecture hard-wires transformer operations instead of retrofitting GPUs, runs at under half the voltage of competing AI chips, and optimizes cluster-scale memory bandwidth because future inference will require thousands of communicating chips, not eight-GPU nodes. Small constraint changes — ignoring sub-zero EDA timing corners data centers never hit — compound into large throughput and cost-per-token gains.

> **F3** Recruiting machine — legends × naive velocity: 'Project-based recruiting' maps the hardest problems in semiconductor history to the people who actually solved them — e.g. Brian Loyler, who built NVIDIA's HGX/DGX systems generating tens of billions per quarter, paired with Gavin's high-school robotics partner Sanford. Philosophy: velocity over outreach, vertical integration over vendors ('best vendor is no vendor'), and willingness to fly engineers to Bangalore for six months when vendors slip a year.

## Mental Model · *Constraint Reset × Vertical Integration × Inference-First Scale*

> **Components**
>
> Incumbent semiconductor and data-center stacks optimize for generality across edge, IoT, and training — leaving inference as retrofit. When the workload is known (transformers), reset assumptions on timing, voltage, interconnect, and rack design simultaneously. Ship full systems, not chips; parallelize chip and rack schedules; hire #1 world experts for impossible problems and pair with hungry generalists who ask why buffers exist.
>
> **Application**
>
> Investors sizing AI infrastructure should separate training capex from inference opex curves — Etched argues decode speed and concurrency cap how many users access frontier models simultaneously. For founders, the lesson is naivety plus first-principles constraint hunting can unlock compounding gains incumbents cannot pursue without cannibalizing general-purpose franchises.

## Key Insights

> **1.** Inference becomes the biggest market in the world.
>
> **Q** Why focus on inference hardware now?
>
> **A** Software COGS is becoming inference-shaped — coding agents and AI-native products need fast decode and massive concurrency. Today only a few million paid AI users exist versus billions potential; serving giant models to billions requires orders-of-magnitude cheaper tokens per watt and cluster-scale memory bandwidth, not training-cluster thinking.

> **2.** General-purpose buffer is the incumbent moat and weakness.
>
> **Q** How can startups beat NVIDIA?
>
> **A** Every layer — EDA defaults, power modules, standard cells — assumes freezing corners and multi-use generality. Etched rejects constraints that do not apply to 80°C data centers and transformer-only math, stacking 20% here and 2× there into system-level wins incumbents cannot adopt without abandoning breadth.

> **3.** The company is a machine that builds machines.
>
> **Q** What matters beyond Sohu v1?
>
> **A** Rob and Gavin emphasize building the organizational machine — parallel rack iterations before silicon returns, thermal dummy chips to validate cold plates pre-tapeout, and a contrarian talent filter selecting engineers willing to bet careers on 10× claims against supply-constrained incumbents.
