## Key Facts

> **F1** Persuasion = desire − fear; belief ≠ trust: LPs can believe a plan is right and still not wire — Kim's skydiving/flying analogy. Friends-and-family is the hard-reelect base (trust already exists); first close often caps near 2× that base — a $1B first close tends to tap out around $2B. Start by mapping who desires your success more than they fear loss, then campaign outward.

> **F2** Three physics laws of fundraising: (1) Differentiation = (track record + differentiation) ÷ complexity of story — complications destroy trust and kill internal advocates who cannot repeat the phrase. (2) Size, speed, and terms trade off — speed is really trust/scarcity; fake scarcity destroys credibility. (3) Pipeline math: pipeline × conversion ratio × bite size — once past hard-reelect, conversion ratio is the only lever that matters.

> **F3** Consensus and courage: Institutional capital hides behind committees that almost never make contrarian bets — build consistency with pensions, consultants, and sovereigns until GC-style consensus forms, then have courage to lose early innovators who say you are too big. Differentiation requires sacrifice — firms that branded 'never weapons' then chased defense lost consistency and their why.

## Mental Model · *Desire−Fear × Trust Velocity × Pipeline Conversion*

> **Components**
>
> Desire and fear are emotional/ethical states; logic rationalizes a decision already felt. Trust is the last unlock after belief. Differentiation must be costly and consistent; complexity of story is the denominator. Campaigns convert a pipeline at a known ratio once the hard-reelect base is mapped. Consensus removes fear for committee capital but forces founders to abandon pure contrarian LP bases.
>
> **Application**
>
> Founders and GPs should audit every pitch for fear in the room, not just IRR slides. Improve conversion by sharpening track record, costly differentiation, and a repeatable one-liner. Never bluff scarcity. When scaling, decide whether size serves a real why — or prepare for the innovator's dilemma of losing early believers.

## Key Insights

> **1.** Money moves at the speed of trust.
>
> **Q** Why do logical pitches still fail?
>
> **A** Belief gets you 'this makes sense'; trust gets the wire. People rationalize risk down rather than being truly risk-loving. Address fear and desire first — logos is the output of a successful meeting, not the input.

> **2.** Differentiation requires sacrifice.
>
> **Q** How do scaled firms keep raising?
>
> **A** Find a durable why for size, not branding. Inconsistent mandates (e.g. never weapons → chase weapons) erase differentiation. Build consensus fund-by-fund with institutional reciprocity, then accept losing innovator LPs who need you small.

> **3.** Fundraisers are secretaries of state.
>
> **Q** Who should represent you?
>
> **A** Unlike cabinet roles with budget control, fundraising faces parties you cannot command — the first impression when you are not in the room must match the principal. Hire for that diplomatic trust, not pure sales energy.
