## Key Facts

> **F1** Animation restart is a CalArts story as much as a CEO story. Room A113 held Lasseter, Brad Bird, Tim Burton, Musker (Little Mermaid/Aladdin/Moana), Stanton, Brenda Chapman (Lion King story), Pete Docter. Little Mermaid cost ≈$40M; Lion King (1994) ≈$45M and $750M box office. Home video becomes a billion-dollar P&L — Lion King tapes ≈$1B with Disney taking roughly ≥50% cash-flow margin (≈$0.5B). Theatrical cash ≈$250–300M plus video puts Lion King near $0.75B in year one. The stage show later tops $11B gross. Parks today ≈$10B operating profit; Euro Disney cost $4B and lost money for years (wine-with-kids lag).

> **F2** Eisner-Wells-Katzenberg: operating profit from well under $300M (1984) to just under $2B a decade later; 1994 market cap $22B, 10x from takeover, ahead of Time Warner/Viacom/Fox. Full Eisner tenure: market cap shy of $2B → ≈$50B; revenue $1.7B → $31B (≈15% CAGR over two decades); net income $97M → $2.5B. After Wells's death, Ovitz lasts 14 months with a $140M exit. 9/11 drops the stock ≈25% and forces most Disney Stores closed.

> **F3** August 1995: Eisner buys Cap Cities/ABC for $19B — then the second-largest deal ever. Buffett/Berkshire was Cap Cities' largest holder (minnow-swallows-whale). ESPN path: Getty startup buy → Texaco → ABC in 1984 with Nabisco, then 20% to Hearst (still held). 1995 ESPN had 66M subs and was only starting the affiliate-fee ramp that later hit $9.42/sub/month. Cable networks later ≈$2B profit versus $4.5B company-wide (≈50%); Ben: over $5B profit from cable channels at the peak machine. Iger arrives as Cap Cities COO.

> **F4** Pixar: Toy Story (Nov 22, 1995) ≈$400M worldwide; IPO day $1.5B cap makes Jobs a billionaire. A Bug's Life $360M. Finding Nemo ≈65M DVDs at ≈$30 (≈$2B home-video gross) plus theatrical ≈ $3B before merch/parks/TV. 2006: Disney pays $7.4B in new stock; Jobs becomes largest Disney shareholder (≈$6.4B net of Pixar cash). Marvel 2009 $4B — later ≈$32B box office across 37 films. Lucasfilm 2012 $4B. 2005–2015: parks revenue nearly doubles, operating income nearly triples; Disney market cap 4x to $200B from ≈$50B. Marvel's $4B was less than one year of 2009 cable profit; Lucasfilm also well under a year. ESPN paid for the three.

> **F5** Aug 4, 2015: Iger cites 'modest subscriber losses'; ESPN lost 3M subs that year — media freak-out with Netflix already ≈$50B cap. BAMTech 33% in Aug 2016 (NHL/HBO Now chops). Netflix license was ≈2% of ≈$15B operating income and they rip it up. Dec 2017 Fox entertainment/international announced at $52B all-stock (no Fox News/Sports/broadcast); Comcast forces $71.3B — $19B extra, ≈40% of Disney's then ≈$170B cap. Net kept assets argued around $44B after sports/news stay with Fox. Disney+ launch price $6.99. Streaming plus Hulu/ESPN tech/marketing loses billions for half a decade while theatrical/DVD/cord fade.

## Mental Model · *Animation Flywheel × Affiliate-Fee Treasury × IP Holdco*

> **Components**
>
> Restart the character engine (CalArts + Broadway + VHS). Use ESPN's forced cable rent to buy Pixar/Marvel/Lucasfilm when those checks are less than a year of cable profit. When the rent disappears (cord-cutting), the same treasury cannot fund a $71B library bid and a $6.99 global streamer without a multi-year earnings hole. Parks remain the physical flywheel Part 1 promised.
>
> **Application**
>
> Underwrite DIS as parks + remaining sports rights + library, not as a Netflix clone. Treat ESPN subscriber losses as the regime change, not a one-quarter print. Fox-at-$71B is the cautionary multiple: winning an auction against CMCSA can be the value-destructive event. Pixar/Marvel-style IP buys only work when a cash gusher (then cable) pays.

---

## What Makes It Work

> Disney's post-Walt edge is a restarted character factory plus a distribution/cash stack peers could not copy on a five-year memo. CalArts supplied the Renaissance; home video and Broadway extracted Lion King-class cash the theatrical split never allowed; parks compounded the same IP into ≈$10B of operating profit. The hidden power from 1995–2015 was ESPN's affiliate fee — $9.42/sub, tens of millions of households, paid whether they watched or not — which funded $7.4B+$4B+$4B of story engines and made Disney the highest-valued traditional media name. That power was counterpositioned against studios that lacked a sports-rent treasury and against tech that lacked a century of affection. It decayed when Netflix-class DTC offered a reason to drop the bundle: the same ESPN that bought Star Wars could not move to $30 direct sports without losing the people who never watched. Fox at $71.3B tried to refill the library for Disney+ and mostly bought a larger streaming P&L. Seven Powers after 1984: branding + scale/network flywheel + cornered multi-studio IP (Pixar/Marvel/Lucasfilm/Fox library) + process power in animation/parks — financed, for two decades, by a cable contract that is not coming back.

## Key Insights

> **1.** The Renaissance was a classroom before it was a CEO.
>
> **Q** Who actually saved animation?
>
> **A** A113 at CalArts — Lasseter, Musker, Stanton, Chapman, Docter — while Eisner-Wells-Katzenberg supplied the commercial machine. Lion King math ($45M / $750M / $1B VHS / $11B stage) is the flywheel in one title.

> **2.** ESPN bought Star Wars.
>
> **Q** How could Disney pay $7.4B+$4B+$4B?
>
> **A** Cable networks were ~half of profit; Marvel's $4B was less than one year of 2009 cable earnings. Affiliate fees, not theatrical luck, funded the holdco.

> **3.** Eisner built the empire and the succession mess.
>
> **Q** What should the 20x tenure be remembered for?
>
> **A** Market cap ≈$2B to ≈$50B and the ABC deal — then Wells's death, Ovitz's $140M, and a board war. Iger inherits both the treasury and the drama.

> **4.** Cord-cutting turns the treasury into a hole.
>
> **Q** Why did 2015 'modest losses' matter?
>
> **A** 3M ESPN subs gone, Netflix already ≈$50B, and the magic of being paid by people who never watch disappears in DTC. Disney+ at $6.99 plus Fox at $71.3B is the expensive answer.

> **5.** Parks are still the Part 1 business.
>
> **Q** What survived the streaming decade?
>
> **A** Parks/experiences ≈$10B operating profit. Films feed the pile; the platform that prints is still physical plus whatever sports rights remain scarce.
