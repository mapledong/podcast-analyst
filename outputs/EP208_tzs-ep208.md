## Key Facts

> **F1** Index calm, sleeve violence. Zhang's cumulative giveback this year is a record for him, even if the absolute number is still controlled. The cold fact: Shanghai Composite amplitude, as he remembers it, is under 20, from a high around 4,200 to a low around 3,600. ChiNext and STAR are about 40% off the late-June high, not quite a halving. A photonics sleeve is more than 50% off the peak. Semiconductors swung hard. The baijiu index's worst drop this year is about 30%. Dividend low-vol, the index that is supposed to be quiet, had a teens drawdown on the year, fell about 10 points in June while tech was frantic, and bounced in July as the market fell. He calls that dividend low-vol turned into dividend high-vol. The push is short-duration money. This week's drop gets blamed on insurers and fixed-income books hitting stop-losses. US AI and tech names, on the same tape, kept making highs. The noise there looks ordinary. A-share old-light names kept falling. Good manufacturers round-tripped whether or not they were tied to AI.

> **F2** Do not grant a long duration. His break was 2023. Looking back, the stable stretch started around 2001: better trade terms outside, a property upcycle inside, household income and wealth rising, and Western value models imported into that weather. He dates the break around 2018, when property topped and the US-China relationship turned. Exports can still be fine. There is no agreed new domestic engine. Households save because the future is unclear. Firms with access raise money. Firms without it cut investment and fight on price. A classic value model that assumes a long calm does not fit a world that will not hand you the duration. Dividend stocks at 6–7% look fine until earnings drop and the dividend itself is the question. He would add some dividend weight. He does not treat 6–7% as a promise.

> **F3** Purchasing power, then the round trip. Over five years Moutai's stock has halved. The index is still above 3,000. Shanghai housing is better. Edge districts can be down by half. High-end prices, including liquor, fell. A drinker who did nothing doubled purchasing power in Moutai. Restaurant purchasing power is up maybe 20–30%. Daily goods barely rose. The thing that hurts can be the thing that later pays, and the reverse. Nvidia and Xiaomi are his paired example of a rally that does not change the market's character: Xiaomi's stock tripled and then came a very large placement. Nvidia ran up and then gave back a large piece. US AI, he notes, had already recovered and was breaking highs again. A-shares stay short-bull, long-bear. He had hoped an AI industrial shift would change that. It has not, much. Li Guofei's old posts name Ping An, Tencent, and Apple and carry the stamp of that era. Zhang says some of his own views rhyme with that blog.

## Mental Model · *Quiet Index × Short-Duration Money × No New Engine*

> **Components**
>
> Shanghai can move less than 20 points from about 4,200 to about 3,600 and still destroy a concentrated book. STAR and ChiNext about 40% off the June high. Photonics more than 50%. Baijiu about 30%. Dividend low-vol down about 10 in the hot June and up in the weak July. Insurers and bond books are the short-duration sellers. The 2001–2018 engine was trade plus property. After that, households save and firms either raise or shrink. A 6–7% dividend is not a duration. Moutai halved and the drinker's purchasing power doubled. Xiaomi's triple ended in a huge placement. Nvidia's rally gave a large piece back and the US tape was at highs again. A-share AI labels did not turn the bull short and the bear long.
>
> **Application**
>
> 600519.SZ is the five-year halving that raised a drinker's purchasing power. NVDA is the US AI name that gave back and then made highs again. 1810.HK is the triple plus a huge placement, the A-share version of a rally that finances itself. Do not read a sub-20 Shanghai amplitude as a quiet book. Underwrite sleeve drawdowns and how short the money is.
