## Background

> Patrick returns with Matthew Smith, founder and CIO of Chronometer Partners (energy, industrials, materials, power/utilities). Smith's team has modeled nearly every US natural-gas well, pipeline, and processing asset — a contrarian conclusion versus a market that still treats gas as plentiful.
>
> The setup predates AI: shale turned the US from importer to LNG exporter; electricity demand stagnated while LNG became the swing demand. Now nameplate LNG export capacity roughly doubles while hyperscalers add gas-hungry generation (fuel cells ≈150 MMcf/d per GW). Shutting exports is not a free option — long-term offtake contracts and the US becoming ≈1/3 of global gas supply lock in volumes.

---

## Key Facts

> **F1** Timing: 2026–27 still look adequately supplied; from 2028, LNG + assigned AI gas loads start eating working-gas storage — the nexus of US supply/demand — with potential exhaustion by ≈2030. Upside risk to gas (and electricity) prices is unbounded and convex. Base AI-related incremental gas ≈5 Bcf/d; extreme path 12–15 Bcf/d by early 2030s.

> **F2** Deliverability, not geology: captured company inventory is largely worked through in ≈4–5 years; max flow modeled ≈128–132 Bcf/d if acreage is drilled optimally — barely enough for committed LNG. Expand Energy may control ≈70% of remaining highly productive inventory; Expand and EQT have mature portfolios where replacement is less costly than five years ago. Midstream gathering/processing is an immediate rate limiter into 2027–28.

> **F3** Winners/losers and nuclear: Utility-scale solar (e.g. Clearway) marks PPAs to higher power prices with little incremental capex; residential solar becomes an economic midday hedge as retail rates rise. Large-scale AP1000-class nuclear is the only durable fix by ≈2033–34; SMRs mostly still science experiments. Consumers lose via bills; Caterpillar/Bloom-style gas gen capacity adds at the wrong time if gas scarcity bites 2028–30.

## Mental Model · *Sources−Uses × Working-Gas Buffer × Convex Electricity*

> **Components**
>
> Match LNG export commitments and AI gas loads against basin deliverability and midstream, not headline reserves. Working-gas storage is the swing buffer — when it depletes, price convexity hits power first. Free-fuel generation (solar/wind) and mature producer inventory capture scarcity rents; new gas turbines amplify scarcity.
>
> **Application**
>
> Stress-test any power or AI-infra thesis for physical gas provisioning through 2028–2030. Prefer producers with mapped engineered inventory and solar assets with merchant/PPA upside over late-cycle distributed gas gen. Treat large nuclear as the multi-year alignment problem for hyperscalers and regulators — not a near-term trade.

## Key Insights

> **1.** The crisis is deliverability on a clock, not running out of molecules.
>
> **Q** Why can't the US just drill more?
>
> **A** Known rock and company acreage support a flow ceiling (≈128–132 Bcf/d) once surface constraints bind. Captured inventory lasts ≈4–5 years; new basins are uneconomic or infrastructure-poor. Exports are contractually sticky — you cannot casually shut LNG to feed data centers.

> **2.** Electricity is where convex gas risk shows up for most people.
>
> **Q** Who feels scarcity first?
>
> **A** Working-gas draws into the late decade push power prices; utility solar re-marks PPAs, residential solar hedges midday retail rates. Gas-turbine and fuel-cell OEM narratives that assume cheap fuel into 2029+ are mis-timed.

> **3.** Solar bridges; large nuclear is the endgame.
>
> **Q** What actually solves the deficit?
>
> **A** Near term only solar/wind displace gas as flex fuel. SMRs lack manufacturing scale for tens of GW; AP1000-class builds need hyperscaler–regulator alignment for 2033–34 COD — belief in the gas problem is the missing catalyst.
