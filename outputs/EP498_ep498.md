## Background

> EP498, Nov 4. Casado has spent about a decade and hundreds of deals on computer-science infrastructure only. He sold Nicira to VMware for $1.26 billion, later sat on the Tabular-to-Databricks sale, and now walks Cursor, World Labs, and Open Router as the recent strategic set. Patrick calls the frame productive investing.

---

## Key Facts

> **F1** Four mindsets, then the shape of a moat. Finance uses the market and the spreadsheet as a proxy for the whole business — SaaS metrics and TAM — and Casado now sees that habit in Series A rooms, which he calls discordant. Productive investors underwrite the asset: technical approach, adjacent budgets, speed, team, partial willingness to pay before COGS is known. Frontier investing is the 1960s version: do not second-guess the founder. Speculators time a market they do not know. Mix the four and any deal can be justified. Separate moat shape from mechanism. Superlinear means $5 million into the slightly larger network pulls away — LinkedIn, and maybe GitHub. Sublinear means the leader grows slower than a challenger spending the same dollar. Uber is funded scale, not a lock. True superlinear cases almost never happen, and defensibility is second-order while the market is still opening white space.

> **F2** Frontier models and the slowdown. For three years the best model has taken 80% or more of dollar-weighted spend, with pricing power for being a bit better and no demand problem. Penetration is still about 0.1% in the broad claim, or 3% of enterprises but 1% or less of budget. 'Never invest in a model' and 'neocloud revenue is bad' both missed value that accrued while the market grew. An Anthropic S-1 buyer still has to ask about defensibility if the thesis is no longer early penetration. Model life, from the app graphs he sees, is three to six months. Software used to have a long tail. A six-month Anthropic slip, or a three-month slip, can lose the market. Coding near a 95% task ceiling slows tokens inside code. It does not license a cheaper model: rivals still pay about 10 times for the smartest one. If use saturates, value can fall into open source. Networking was sleepy for 15 years; clusters are producing the first new companies since Arista, plus optical.

> **F3** Private markets, then Cursor. His dream book is 5% of every competitive round — he names Cognition and Cursor — because private markets look efficient and the deals that returned capital were the competitive ones. You cannot index them. The firm picks the top company in each real sector and takes the market price. Hunting non-consensus, he says, adds follow-on risk. He rejects the line that only about 20 startups matter. Nicira to VMware was $1.26 billion, about a $1 billion option on a $40 billion market. Cursor is the largest of the recent strategic set in his account. A few-billion raise was nearly done when a model he prices at about $3 billion to train made compute the bottleneck. SpaceX had the compute. Cursor had the users and the data. He pairs Michael Truell with Elon. Open Router leaked. World Labs did not. Strategic M&A is trust, not a poker game.

## Mental Model · *Four Mindsets × Moat Shape × Three-to-Six-Month Models*

> **Components**
>
> Name the mindset before the price. Finance reads TAM too early. Productive investing maps a technical approach onto how infrastructure is bought. Frontier investing does not second-guess the founder. Speculation times a chart. Moat shape is superlinear, linear, or sublinear, separate from the mechanism. LinkedIn is the rare superlinear case. Uber is funded scale. While the market grows, defensibility is second-order. Frontier dollars at 80% or more for three years sit on about 1% budget penetration. Model life is three to six months. A coding ceiling slows tokens and still does not license a dumber model. Private markets are efficient enough that the job is the best company per sector. Extra capital enlarges the set. Strategic sales clear on trust and an option on a larger market.
>
> **Application**
>
> Private:Anthropic is the S-1 the tape says a public buyer must underwrite for defensibility — 80% dollar share while growth holds, and a six-month slip that can zero the last model. Private:Cursor is the strategic-sale file: users and data against a compute owner, price set by a few-billion raise, trust instead of a leak. ANET is the last networking platform's public winner and the name he uses for 'nothing new since,' while he also says an incumbent can add optical and cluster features. Do not treat a sponsor read as a holding. Do not short a lab because coding tasks look saturated.

## Key Insights

> **1.** Name the mindset or any deal clears.
>
> **Q** What are the four?
>
> **A** Spreadsheet finance, productive assets, 1960s founder bets, and market timing. Finance is showing up at Series A. Mix them and bias writes the memo.

> **2.** A smarter model still gets the dollar.
>
> **Q** Does 95% coding saturation cheapen tokens?
>
> **A** No. Token growth inside code can slow. Competitive equilibrium still pays about 10 times for the smartest model. Life of a model is three to six months; 80% or more of dollars have sat at the frontier for three years.

> **3.** The Cursor sale was an option on compute.
>
> **Q** What cleared it?
>
> **A** A few-billion raise was setting price. A model he costs at about $3 billion needed compute SpaceX had. Cursor had users and data. Leaks killed the feel of Open Router; World Labs stayed quiet.
