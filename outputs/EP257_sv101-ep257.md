## Key Facts

> **F1** The S-1 is the cold open, not a footnote. Anthropic filed this week. Yiwen counts nearly 100 pages of risk factors, including catastrophic and existential risk from frontier AI, plus a line that the company cannot promise its safety measures will work. Ctrl-F: safety 248 times, AI safety 36 times. After listing, seven co-founders keep 50.1% of votes on ordinary matters through a special share class. A long-term benefit trust may elect a majority of the board, so commercial goals are supposed to sit next to humanity's long-run interest. Founders may pre-name who inherits their votes if they cannot serve. Those names need internal approval and stay secret. Yiwen's second worry is governance: if the warning is real, the public still does not know who might speak for humanity. That stack is why the episode treats AI safety as a corporate fact about Anthropic, not only a philosophy seminar. Holden Karnofsky, married to Daniela Amodei, co-founded GiveWell and Open Philanthropy.

> **F2** How EA got to the labs. MacAskill, an Oxford philosopher, asks how limited money and time can help more people, and co-founded 80,000 Hours — a career is about that many hours. SBF made 'earn to give' famous; the FTX collapse brought the criticism, and did not end the movement. Yudkowsky is the rationalist who asked how a smarter-than-human machine stays aimed at human interests. Dario Amodei left OpenAI with colleagues to start Anthropic over safety and direction. Vitalik funded global health and, in 2021, sent crypto to the Future of Life Institute. He later recalled that gift realizing about $500 million, and he has moved away from some of that circle's methods. The camps Afra draws are no longer one EA: EA close to Anthropic, EA far from Anthropic, Pause AI (more populist, willing to say rationalism and AI are bad), and e/acc — Andreessen Horowitz and Jeff Bezos — which wants acceleration. Rationalists sit nearby but, in Vitalik's split, they are motivated by curiosity; EA is solemn, a rescue mission that does not get to play.

> **F3** d/acc against the OpenAI sequence. Vitalik's label is defensive, decentralized or democratizing, and acceleration. Technology should keep moving. More of the budget should go to protection: cybersecurity against AI hacking, and antiviral work before models make dangerous biology easier. The third leg refuses concentrated power — open source and tools that empower ordinary people, with a Switzerland-and-mountains picture of small independent units rather than one capital. Defensive and democratizing reinforce each other. Afra's short history of OpenAI is the foil: they sacrificed openness for safety (GPT-3 too dangerous to open-source, around 2020, plus safety pledges), then sacrificed safety for acceleration when the pledges, including ones to government bodies, became inconvenient. Altman pushed out and brought back is, on this tape, one more round of e/acc versus EA inside the same company. Vitalik offers d/acc as acceleration that does not hand the vote to a secret list. The named firms are Anthropic, OpenAI, and a16z.
