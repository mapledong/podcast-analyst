## Background

> Patrick sits with Sam Altman for a wide arc across OpenAI's past year (tough, partly self-inflicted) and the next twelve months (possibly their best). Topics include early compute allocation, chip fabs and energy outreach that sounded crazy at the time, Kimi/distillation and China-frontier dynamics, the Hugging Face incident, Codex's product race, and new hardware ambitions beyond the 50-year keyboard-mouse-monitor paradigm.
>
> The conversation keeps returning to abundance: drive cost of intelligence down the stack (electricity → chips → systems → products) so useful AI is cheap and everywhere, while still pushing the frontier.

---

## Key Facts

> **F1** Compute thesis: securing early, large compute was historically unusual and still bottlenecks OpenAI; research quality scales with compute so 'ideas vs GPUs' are coupled — a training run can rival the entire compute budget from 18 months prior. Energy through a major cluster can power a small city; outreach to chip fabs and energy providers once looked irrational.

> **F2** Economics and distillation: much future compute is planned to sell inference; even modest margins on a huge revenue bucket can fund the stack. Altman has not deeply resolved distillation/China copy dynamics, but argues frontier returns and customer inference demand still dominate; people fear 'cheating' by distillation while the bigger game is scale of intelligence sold.

> **F3** Products and hardware: Codex is winning (in his view) via best product + model, not lock-in — users can leave if someone is better; durable edge is compute-fleet scale and cheapest intelligence. Custom efficient chips (Jalapeño) target specific workflows. Current devices are poor for always-on, proactive, context-rich AI — OpenAI is exploring socially acceptable always-listening hardware. Kids will never grow up smarter than computers.

## Mental Model · *Abundant Intelligence Stack × Compute Fleet Moat × Distill-Aware Frontier*

> **Components**
>
> Optimize the full cost stack from power and silicon to products. Treat large owned/partnered compute as the hard-to-copy asset while product skins stay contestable. Keep pushing frontier quality even when distillation compresses mid-tier pricing. Redesign hardware so AI can be proactive without living inside a 1970s I/O paradigm.
>
> **Application**
>
> AI investors should separate fleet/energy/chip bets from app-layer churn. Stress-test OpenAI-adjacent names on inference volume and power delivery, not just model demos. Watch Codex-like coding agents as the first mass workflow where compute-fleet advantage shows up in unit economics. Treat always-on hardware experiments as optional upside, not the core thesis.

## Key Insights

> **1.** The job is abundant, cost-effective intelligence — not a single chat product.
>
> **Q** What is OpenAI optimizing for now?
>
> **A** Best + most abundant + cheapest useful intelligence across the stack. That reframes chips, energy, Codex, and hardware as one system rather than side projects.

> **2.** Compute fleets are more durable than any product skin.
>
> **Q** Where is the moat if Codex users can leave?
>
> **A** Altman says Codex wins on product/model quality today, but lasting advantage is scale of compute and ability to make more of it. Workflow lock-in is weaker than fleet economics.

> **3.** Kids will never know a world without abundant intelligence.
>
> **Q** Why does parenthood matter to the thesis?
>
> **A** Personal stakes reinforce the abundance agenda: raise children who grow up with computers smarter than them as normal — so the mandate is to make that intelligence cheap, useful, and widely available.
