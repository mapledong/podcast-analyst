## Key Facts

> **F1** Surface and school: reporters in the early 1980s find the richest man in America is not Rockefeller, DuPont, Trump, Kennedy, Getty, or Perot but an unglamorous guy from Arkansas. Depression Missouri, college, WWII stateside, Oklahoma banker's daughter, first five-and-dime in north-central Arkansas, four kids. At JCPenney in 1940 the chain has over 1,500 stores and $300 million in sales. John Cash Penney, then 65, watches Sam wrap a box and rewrap it with less paper. Customer satisfaction ahead of profits. Strength is small towns — the map Walmart later copies. Sam will wander stores the way Penney wandered his.

> **F2** Newport and restart: he builds a Ben Franklin into the best in the region, then the landlord and family steal the location. He finds the store and cannot buy the building. Personality: my store will be here anyway. He takes the lesson and the money to a new town and does it again. Tiny stores first. The first five-and-dime is a farming-town box, not a city flagship. Managers must know their numbers. He spends days walking competitors. Ideas that later put billions on the scoreboard start as sidewalk traffic tricks and borrowed discount math.

> **F3** How he worked: fly himself, Saturday morning meetings, steal any merchandising idea that moves goods, stay in small towns until the map is dense, then hit the cities. Trimble is almost 40 years old and predates the official autobiography — Senra wants the working habits, not the myth polish. Kmart and the catalog giants are the named rivals he studies in person. The richest-man reveal is the punchline; the method is the product.

## Mental Model · *Small-Town Density × Steal the Idea × Saturday Morning*

> **Components**
>
> Look ordinary. Work like a maniac. Learn wrapping paper from the 65-year-old founder. Lose the building and start again. Put managers on numbers. Walk the other guy's aisle. Saturday is for the system, not rest. Density in forgotten towns beats a flagship. Autobiography is later; habits are now. Keep the method boring enough to repeat every week. Numbers in the room beat stories in the hallway. The founder who will not walk the floor, make the tenth call, or rewrite the Saturday software is already the cartel.
>
> **Application**
>
> WMT is the named compounder: small-town first, Penney's 1,500-store map inverted. JCP is the 1940 classroom ($300M, 1,500 stores, wrap the box). TGT is the later city-discount foil he would have walked. Fade 'ordinary Arkansas' as the thesis. Size any retailer that will not walk stores on Saturday as already losing.
