## Key Facts

> **F1** The export numbers. Across third-party counts, Korean beauty sales more than doubled globally in two years. The US is now the largest export destination. Nearly one in four American households has used a Korean product. Western Europe is growing faster than North America. Exports to the UK rose about 1.8 times in the first three quarters. APR, a newer group, grew European revenue about 380% in the second quarter. Korea has passed the US and sits second only to France as a beauty exporter. The price that surprises people is low: about 100 to 200 yuan, or $10 to $50, for products that sound technical. Kelly and Han Xiao say the heat is not the price. It is the control of a marketing concept, a state policy that sends culture out to carry the product, and a supply chain that leaves as a group rather than as one brand.

> **F2** Old groups and new labels. The 1.0 brands and the 2.0 brands are partly the same companies. Amorepacific, founded in 1945, still has Sulwhasoo, Laneige, and Innisfree, and the 2.0 snail line COSRX, and it has been buying newer names such as Aestura. LG Household and Health is the other old giant that is still here. The new fortunes are often one group with a long list of small brands, each brand built around one hero product. Medicube is the example they use. Centralized media made Estee Lauder, L'Oreal, and Helena Rubinstein, and also Chinese names such as Proya. That broadcast is hard to repeat, outside elevator ads people hate. The new Korean labels spread through circles, then through US shelves at Sephora and Costco and through Olive Young at home. Local American influencers, not only Korean celebrities, now carry the product. Kendall Jenner is one of the endorsements they mention.

> **F3** The distributor that used to sell chips. Silicon2 is the firm Mahua found organizing the overseas shelf, especially in Europe and, in the conversation, the US network. It was founded in 2002 as a semiconductor trader. In 2012, as Korean beauty was growing, it switched to cosmetics distribution. It is now one of the larger Korean beauty distributors. US warehouses mean the box does not have to come through Hong Kong, which changes time and cost versus peers that only store in Hong Kong. It works with more than 430 Korean brands. That is the group exit: culture, a pile of hero SKUs, and a distributor that already knows how to move physical goods across a border because it used to move chips. The Western incumbents on the tape are the brands of the broadcast era. They are not the ones opening the US warehouse.
