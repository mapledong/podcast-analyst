## Key Facts

> **F1** What counts as xia. Liang treats hatred and grievance as the fuel of the whole wuxia genre, the thing that separates it from other stories. Nothing pushes harder. Xia is the extreme form of keeping your own mind while that pressure is on you. The difference from a good person is the size of the will. Helping a grandmother across the street is ordinary goodness. Pulling her out of traffic when bystanders may say you knocked her down is the version that costs something. He put the moral in the script once, lines in the key of a great xia protecting ordinary people. Most players rejected it. Not the ethic. They saw the author's intention. The 16 characters a colleague used for the game are his modern compression of the old plot: wronged, fugitive, alone inside a rigged board, hatred running to the end.

> **F2** No person is the enemy. The boss is the market, the objective world, the thing you have to fight whether or not you have a villain. Liang describes himself as a small-town exam kid. The enemy was the paper in front of him. Genius, he says, is unreliable. Effective makers are few. At 20 the wuxia trap is the flashy talent, beautiful technique, who never reaches the great city. The ones who do look a little slow. Guo Jing, not the brilliant cousin. A young man who has just made money will not say plainly that the company has to profit in order to make a better game. He wanted the world to recognize him. Later he will say the plain sentence.

> **F3** The company had to exist. A personal creator inside a big-company KPI, a weekly report, and someone else's culture hits a structural paradox. Liang thinks that kind of person has to become a founder or the work cannot be finished. Big companies are still good. Tencent is a shareholder and, he says, nice, and has helped many people. The relationship is the wuxia one: the wandering fighter and the regular army, or the pirate and the navy in One Piece. The money started before the company. In August 2011 Xu Xiaoping, through ZhenFund, wired 1 million yuan to Liang's personal account. Almost no terms. The company, Lingyoufang, was registered that month, and the personal money was later converted into shares. Years afterward Liang bought that piece back for 30 million yuan and calls it a good little case for Xu. A ZhenFund vice president joined in September 2011. NetEase came around 2013, as a publishing check, in the decade when Chinese mobile games were the primitive accumulation and, he also says, a black history. NetEase was starting its own mobile games at the same time. The team he describes for that period is about 20 people. Phantom Blade, the mobile game, shipped around 2013. The console ambition was already in his head when it was announced, and the direction has not been replaced. Departed colleagues asked to be named in the credits.

## Key Insights

> **1.** Ordinary goodness is not xia.
>
> **Q** What is the grandmother test?
>
> **A** Walking her across an empty street proves you are a decent person. It does not take extraordinary will. Pulling her out of moving traffic when the crowd may decide you caused the fall is the version Liang will call xia. Hatred and grievance are the fuel. Keeping a human mind under that fuel is the result. He tried putting the moral in the dialogue. Players saw the intention and rejected the line, not the ethic. A colleague's 16-character brief is the same fuel in shorter form: wronged, on the run, alone inside a rigged maze, hatred at the end. Those words start the plot. They do not finish the character. The playable question is whether he stays human while using them. Liang will not count a riskless kindness. He will count the help that can be misread as guilt. That is why a slogan in a cutscene fails and a costly action can still work. The audience is not rejecting mercy. It is rejecting the moment it can see the writer asking for credit.

> **2.** The brilliant cousin does not finish.
>
> **Q** Who reaches the great city?
>
> **A** Wuxia is full of beautiful technique that never becomes mastery. Liang's finisher looks a little dull. Guo Jing, not the flashy relative. He says real genius is unreliable and fragile, and that distinct makers are rarer than effective ones. Gu Long does not have many copies. A young man who has just made money will not say out loud that the company must profit. Later he will. At 20 the point of the money is to be seen. The later sentence is plainer: profit is what lets the next game exist. The dull finisher is the one who can say that without turning it into a pose.

> **3.** The navy can be a nice shareholder.
>
> **Q** How did the company start?
>
> **A** August 2011, 1 million yuan into a personal account, almost no terms, company registered the same month. The buyback was 30 million. Tencent is a shareholder he calls nice. NetEase's publishing money arrived around 2013, when the team was about 20 people. A founder who needs a KPI and a weekly report is, in his view, inside a paradox. The pirate still has to live with the navy. August 2011 is the personal account. The company is registered the same month. The 30 million buyback is years later. NetEase is 2013, about 20 people, mobile Phantom Blade. Tencent stays a shareholder. None of those facts replaces the author. They are how the author keeps a structure that can finish a long game.
