## Key Facts

> **F1** The spread business the banks dropped. Hongjun's picture is an airport exchange desk: a bid, an offer, a thin gap. Market maker and high-frequency shop are the same people, machines and models. Goldman and Jane Street sit one street apart in Manhattan. Goldman pay is about $400,000 a person. Jane Street pay, from the Bloomberg report she cites, is about $2.68 million. Banks once ignored electronic market making because the profit looked too thin. After 2008 the Volcker rule limited proprietary trading with the bank's own money. Electronics did the rest. Twenty years later these firms are the new whales. Jane Street's edge was ETFs. That market was about $70 billion when it entered and about $24 trillion now. This year Jane Street signed about $19 billion of multi-year compute. XTX is also spending heavily on compute. Several of these firms were early investors in frontier model labs.

> **F2** XTX, High-Flyer, and the anti-human rule. XTX was founded around the same time as OpenAI and bet early on deep learning. It was an early investor in Anthropic. It once shared a campus with DeepMind. A Financial Times report from about two years ago, dated December 2024 on the tape, put the firm at a bit over 200 people and about 25,000 GPUs. Last year three UK operating entities had about £3.93 billion of net revenue. Public filings imply nearly $20 million of revenue per employee, which Hongjun calls the highest among quant shops. Founder Alex Gerko came from FX. The hiring joke is the point: a researcher who steadily loses a few thousand of his own money, and more in a bad year, is hired because he will not override the model with a personal view. His boss says that personal judgment is nothing. High-Flyer, cofounded by DeepSeek's Liang Wenfeng, is the Chinese firm from the same era. DRW, the other guest's old shop, started in interest-rate derivatives and was in the market when Lehman failed in 2008.

> **F3** Why the strategy is called anti-human. The product is not a star trader. It is a model that must not be talked out of a print by someone who is sure this time is different. The compensation gap, $400,000 versus $2.68 million, is what that model is worth to the people who keep it running. The compute bills, $19 billion at Jane Street and a large GPU pile at XTX, are the same bet the labs are making, funded by a spread instead of a subscription. ETFs supplied Jane Street's volume. Deep learning supplied XTX's. The regulatory gift was Volcker. None of these firms is a public stock on this tape except the bank that no longer owns the book. Goldman is the before picture, one street away and much cheaper per person.
