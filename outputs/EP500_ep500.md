## Key Facts

> **F1** Share of GDP is the wrong complaint. The US spends 18% of GDP on healthcare and people call that the problem. He asks why an absolute share is a problem if the society is buying health. Jevons, in his use, says the market gets larger when the unit cost falls. US unit costs have been rising, which is why 18% feels broken. About 50% of the cost sits on 5% of people, because the US does not give up on that person. The UK healthcare share went from 6% to 12% of GDP, Germany from 9% to 12%, the US from 9% to 18%. Obesity moved with it, and GLP-1s are why the US obesity chart has flattened. Follow the money: about 30-35% hospitals, 20-25% doctors' offices, 9-10% drugs. Acute care is better than the criticism. Cancer, heart attack, and stroke survival beat the NHS and other OECD systems. The US does not use more care: fewer and shorter hospital stays, a few more scans, then higher prices and a much higher administrative load.

> **F2** Prices are not prices. A $1,300 list drug can be $300 to $400 behind the scenes. No other industry, he says, could survive with prices that are not public. PBMs and insurers sit between the patient and the price, and list prices step up 6, 7, or 8% a year. Cash pay, or insurance that covers 100% and still leaves choice, is where patients feel in control. Ro's idea is to aggregate patient spend until the system has to face the patient. Remove the PBM and the insurer from the GLP-1 path and the $1,300 price is not the price the patient should see. Per person, the system spends on the order of $20,000 to $25,000 a year. Employers write one check for many employees. The patient writes none of it and is not the customer.

> **F3** Ro's four-year print, and the GLP-1 versus AI chart. Four categories. Cardiometabolic is first, then sexual health, dermatology, and fertility. Ro is a leading US GLP-1 channel and is integrated with both Novo and Lilly. Run rate went from about $250 million to over $3 billion in four years, with fewer people than in 2021. Critics call that just weight-loss drugs. The labels moved from diabetes to obesity, then heart disease, sleep apnea, liver disease, and kidney disease, with more studies behind them. Patrick cites a chart from a few days before the taping: GLP-1 revenue is larger than AI revenue, and about twice as many individuals pay for a GLP-1 as pay for AI. Reitano reads that as consumers reporting daily impact, and as the hole in the AI story. A new medical technology does not get a free pass. It has to be substantially better than the status quo, not 1% better. He uses driverless cars the same way: blocking a safer option should carry a body count. His worry is that healthcare will keep scoring inputs instead of outcomes.

>
> **Application**
>
> Private:Ro is the cash-pay aggregator, not an insurer. LLY and NVO are the two supply sources on the tape. Do not short the healthcare share of GDP because 18% looks large. Underwrite unit cost and whether the patient can see the price. A 1% better input is not the bar he wants for a new therapy.
