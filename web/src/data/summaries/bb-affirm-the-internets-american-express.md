## Key Facts

> **F1** Network math. Average purchase is about $250. A new user gives mobile, name, DOB, last four of SSN; Affirm matches basket and credit file, then locks a fixed rate and a plan that pays to zero. Merchant gets $250 minus MDR (example $5). About half of loans sell off; half stay on balance sheet. Mix: ≈70% interest-bearing, the rest split 0% merchant-funded and pay-in-X. Pay-in-X and 0% make money on MDR; interest-bearing is mostly consumer interest. Gross profit after losses and funding: pay-in-X 1–3% of GMV, interest-bearing 4–6%, blended about 4%. Card mix is even more interest-bearing. Since IPO, usage went from two transactions a year to seven; in any quarter ≈95% of volume is existing users. Cash sales-and-marketing has barely moved since 2021. CAC through a typical merchant button is negative.

> **F2** Share, card, funding. US e-commerce is ≈20% of retail; US BNPL is ≈6% of e-commerce versus 15–25% in Australia/Sweden/Europe. Affirm has ≈50% US BNPL share and is gaining — ≈2x Klarna in the US. Card (Visa rails, debit/credit toggle) is ≈20% of users and GMV and still growing over 100% year on year, with unit economics at least as good as the blended book. Loss rates: about 3.5% on monthly installments versus ≈1% on pay-in-X. Affirm is ≈50% prime and still better than revolving books because every ticket is underwritten. Funding is ≈50/50 off/on balance sheet; they are applying for a bank charter. Average loan life ≈11–12 months: 50% of the book is on the new rate in one quarter, 75% in two. +100 bps is about $90 million against ≈$2.5 billion of gross profit this year. The 2022 air pocket was one ugly December quarter, then two quarters of repair. 0-to-5% (500 bps) is the bite: they stop writing 15% loans that do not work at 25%.

> **F3** Size, drawdown, Max. Listed ≈$49, day-one almost $100, ≈$175 a year later, then $9 in the hike — more than a 90% drawdown. Now roughly $60–80, about $25 billion fully diluted, ≈20x adjusted earnings (SBC stripped). GMV ≈$50 billion, +30%, RLTC ≈$2 billion; they talk $2.5 billion of GP this June year. Target: $100 billion GMV at >25% from core US, card, and international; Edge and agents sit on top. US card balances are ≈$1.3 trillion versus Affirm's ≈$10 billion book; Visa/Mastercard/Amex credit volume is ≈$13 trillion a year. Amazon is over 20%; long contracts with Amazon and Shopify. Walmart switched to Klarna OnePay; most of those shoppers still pay with the Affirm card, and it was a low-profit logo. G&A flat ≈5 years, SBC flat ≈6 years. Max cash salary under $40,000, no cash bonus, a couple of billion of stock; two presidents own $100 million-plus each. 70–80% of code already ships via AI. Shop Pay installments is Affirm white-label.

## Mental Model · *Internet Amex × Ticket-by-Ticket Credit × Two Pac-Mans*

> **Components**
>
> Treat Affirm as American Express (network plus credit), not Visa rails and not a virtual-card lender. The checkout button is the flywheel: more merchants, more negative-CAC users, more volume for the next merchant. Underwrite every $250 basket — not a $7,000 revolving line — and pay the plan to zero with no late fees or compounding. ≈70% interest-bearing is why blended GP/GMV is ≈4% after losses and funding. Two share shifts: e-commerce still ≈20% of US retail, BNPL still ≈6% of US e-commerce versus 15–25% abroad. Card extends the network into stores and feeds the enterprise sale. An 11–12 month book turns a rate shock into a one-to-two-quarter air pocket, not a revolving washout. HVF is why they started on Peloton-class loans and backfilled pay-in-four.
>
> **Application**
>
> AFRM is the name: $50 billion GMV, +30%, ≈50% US BNPL, path to $100 billion with Edge and agents extra. Size it as a network that still clears ≈20x after a 90% drawdown, not as Synchrony. AXP is the analog (credit is why Amex never got the Visa/Mastercard tape). SHOP is Shop Pay installments and one of two logos that took equity. Amazon >20% is the concentration risk; Walmart showed logo loss can be survivable if the card keeps the user. Fade 'subprime kids' — dollars skew 30-plus. Do not treat +50–100 bps as 2022. Agents help a 0% merchant-funded offer more than a 20% revolving card.

## Key Insights

> **1.** It is Amex, not a virtual card.
>
> **Q** Lender multiple or network multiple?
>
> **A** Direct merchant integrations plus the loan. 95% repeat volume and 2→7 uses/year are the flywheel Visa-style issuers do not have.

> **2.** 70% interest-bearing is the P&L.
>
> **Q** Does the card wreck unit economics?
>
> **A** Card mix is even more interest-bearing. Blended GP/GMV stays ≈4% after losses and funding.

> **3.** 2022 was one quarter, not a credit wipeout.
>
> **Q** What does a hike actually do?
>
> **A** An 11–12 month book reprices in one to two quarters. They stop writing loans that do not clear the new rate. Revolving cards wash out; Affirm slows.
