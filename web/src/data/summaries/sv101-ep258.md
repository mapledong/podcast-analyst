## Key Facts

> **F1** The house versus the spray. HF0 admits 10 companies a batch and runs 3 batches a year. They prefer repeat founders. Hongjun's compare is YC at about 200 companies a batch and 4 batches a year. Joe has not been to YC. He still says a founder holding both offers picks HF0 without hesitating. YC is a brand and, in his joke, spray and pray: water everyone and hope someone grows. HF0 is selective, the acceptance rate is far below YC, and the work is living and working in one place for 3 months. The term moved as the program got hotter. Early checks were $250,000 for 2.5%. The batch before ACE was $625,000 for 3%. ACE's fall 2024 batch was $1 million for 5%. Investors had told Hongjun that clever people in one house for 3 months produce new ideas, and that some companies 10x inside those months.

> **F2** OpenRouter, one year ahead. Joe places OpenRouter in the fall 2023 batch, 12 months before ACE. Two cofounders then. Hongjun's other figure is 4 people, 2 of them part-time, when it entered. The host had heard a multi-billion-dollar sale. On the tape the guest's next line is that the recent story is not a Stripe acquisition. What they do describe is the product. OpenRouter wanted to be the router across models: faster, more stable, more models. The first-mover spot became the place global developers test a new model, and the call-count chart became an authority. Labs then asked to be listed, instead of OpenRouter begging them. They did not push commercialization or a fundraise once that slot was clear. The price is the model price plus 5%. They do not run the China-style spread of buying at 30% and selling at 50%. New model releases are their growth, not a weekly influencer trick. Joe says that is a different growth from ACE.

> **F3** Growth as a product, not a trick. ACE makes a voice model for working musicians. Joe names Hans Zimmer, Teddy Riley, and other Grammy-level users. Inside the house the question every week is the growth number. Some weeks he had to stand up and say there was no growth while everyone else had some. A 10% week and a 50% week both showed up. Small tricks, including influencer growth that other teams were famous for, do not hold a stable curve. He came in ignorant of growth and left with other people asking ACE to teach it. The line he keeps is to build growth the way you build a product, or the way you build an algorithm. Hongjun had wanted the management lesson for AI companies: which HF0 habits travel after the batch. Joe's answer is the weekly number, the refusal to blame everything on growth, and the 10x the batch actually printed for them. OpenRouter is the exception he sets aside, because a router can grow when a new model ships even if the team does little that week.

## Mental Model · *Ten-Company House × 5% Check × Growth as a Product*

> **Components**
>

OpenRouter, fall 2023, four people, takes 5% on the token price and became the test door for new models. ACE, fall 2024, treats weekly growth as a product. Tricks do not hold. A week of zero is part of the room. A sale rumor from the host is not the operating fact on the tape. That is the tell.

Private:ACE Studio is the music-voice company that 10x'd inside the house. Do not underwrite a Stripe price. Underwrite whether the growth survives the week the trick stops.

## Key Insights

> **1.** Founders pick the small house.
>
> **Q** Why HF0 over YC?
>
> **A** Joe says they do not hesitate. YC is a brand and a spray of about 200 teams. HF0 is 10 teams living together, and the acceptance rate is far lower.

> **2.** The check got more expensive as the program worked.
>
> **Q** What did ACE give up?
>
> **A** Early batches were $250,000 for 2.5%, then $625,000 for 3%. His fall 2024 batch was $1 million for 5%.

> **3.** OpenRouter grows when a model ships. ACE had to build the week.
>
> **Q** What is the 5%?
>
> **A** OpenRouter adds 5% to the model price and does not run a discount spread. It became the first door for testing a new model. The ACE 10x was the house, not that router math.
