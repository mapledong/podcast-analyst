## Key Facts

> **F1** Track: launch 2001-09-10, close early 2014, ≈13 years, 10.2× / 20.8% annualized vs MSCI World. 2001–07 half-year max loss ≤5%; after 2008 vol and returns both rose. Two people only — Nick (≈1968, UK, into Marathon in 1995 via Hosking) and Zack (Iraqi, UK 1970, Cambridge math, Jardine/Deutsche Asia through the 1996–99 bust). No sell-side, one Bloomberg, no leverage/derivatives. Early book hunted cheap assets from Asia to Zimbabwe; 2003 top-10 names were ≈40%.

> **F2** 2004–06 thought shift: stop paying 50¢ for $1 of replacement-cost assets and instead pay $1 for a firm that can become $2/$4/$8 via shared scale economies (Rabasin’s ratio on who captures incremental surplus — customer, employee, shareholder). Costco was the teaching stock: EDLP, low markup, Sinegal culture versus Walmart/Target/Sears. Amazon from 2005, ≈20% by 2006, to ≈40% in the 2007–08 ≈50% drawdown, then +30% after 2012 reopened to inflows — effectively buy-only. ASOS 2009 ≈6% then 10%+; Ocado added into a near-halving; AirAsia one of the few full exits then buybacks (treated as a cycle). Costco saw a large trim then a 2012 add back to the 2008 peak weight.

> **F3** Process: first buys already large (ASOS/Ocado ≈7%, AirAsia 4% as a top outside holder). Trim a little into strength (tax), add on any meaningful dip if cash is there. Advantages Toby lists: no information edge, an analysis edge (shared-scale template), and a psychology edge (Nick’s Placebo/bridge talk). Close ≈2014 → personal capital/philanthropy because they felt the craft had no more room — the room’s critique is they then missed a decade of tech/AI/bitcoin iteration. Don’t photocopy the template into a different market.

## Mental Model · *Shared Scale × Buy-Only Adds × Paradigm Expiry*

> **Components**
>
> Pay up for firms that share surplus with customers and still scale. Size the first ticket so a crash can be doubled (Amazon 20%→40%). Closing a fund can freeze the last good idea. Keep a capital-cycle cheap-asset toolkit for when the compounder set is empty.
>
> **Application**
>
> AMZN/COST still map to the shared-scale slide. Use 2008-style adds only if the thesis is scale, not a cycle (AirAsia). Treat a ‘we are done iterating’ manager as a sell signal on the process, not a reason to copy 2014 holdings forever.

## Key Insights

> **1.** The 2004–06 pivot is the whole story.
>
> **Q** What changed Nomad from a cheap-asset fund?
>
> **A** Shared scale economies — pay $1 for a firm that can become $8 — after Costco/Amazon teaching, not 50¢ replacement-cost spreads.

> **2.** Amazon was a buy-only tape.
>
> **Q** How did they use the 2007–08 halving?
>
> **A** 20% became 40%, then another 30% after 2012 inflows. That is the operating rule, not a slogan.

> **3.** Closing the fund froze the paradigm.
>
> **Q** What did 2014 cost them?
>
> **A** They felt the craft was finished and went to philanthropy — and therefore did not have to metabolize tech/AI.
