## Key Facts

> **F1** Origin myth: Musk's Mars Oasis plan to buy a Soviet missile, plant a greenhouse on Mars, and shame NASA into funding — three Russia trips ended in humiliation; Excel math showed materials ≈3% of rocket price, so he founded SpaceX in 2002 with PayPal cash (eBay's $1.5B all-cash deal), earlier than Facebook and years before iPhone 4. Falcon 1 was built in a tiny LA warehouse beside the airport.

> **F2** Three early Falcon 1 failures nearly killed the company amid the financial crisis and Tesla stress; Ashlee Vance-sourced pep talk lore: SpaceX would not die. Dec 23, 2008 NASA phone call — $1.6B COTS-class lifeline. Falcon 9 reuse matured toward ≈100% success, boosters past 20 flights, turnaround under 20 days; Feb 21, 2026 a booster completed its 33rd flight/landing, crushing the old '10 flights is physics' folklore. Crew Dragon became the US's practical crew vehicle as Boeing's crewed plan slipped indefinitely after Crew-9 optics.

> **F3** Starship materials bet: stainless (V-2/Atlas heritage) is cheap, heat/cold tolerant, and factory-friendly versus pristine carbon-fiber cleanrooms costing years and hundreds of millions. Culture: Musk's five-step method plus 'prove it' dissent — not blind kingship. Mission remains Mars colonization / extending consciousness beyond Earth.

## Key Insights

> **1.** SpaceX is a redemption loop, not a clean S-curve.
>
> **Q** Why did it survive 2008?
>
> **A** Technical near-misses plus a NASA contract at the last dollar — commercial space needed a buyer of first resort.

> **2.** Reuse turned folklore into compounding CAPEX.
>
> **Q** What's the real metric?
>
> **A** Flights per booster and days between flights beat brochure Isp; 33 recoveries reprice the industry cost curve.

> **3.** Starship manufacturing is the product.
>
> **Q** Why steel tents?
>
> **A** Cheap material + outdoor factories beat aerospace cleanroom CapEx when the vehicle is 100m class.
