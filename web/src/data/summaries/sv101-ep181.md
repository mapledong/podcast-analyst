## Key Facts

> **F1** Four systems: skeleton (Al/Ti at knees/ankles for impact), joints/actuators (rotary = motor+reducer; linear = motor+ballscrew), sensors (force/torque, cameras, IMU with accel/gyro/mag fusion), electrical/compute. Flips impose crash-like accelerations — early Atlas hydraulics gave way to motor joints that finally enable dance/kungfu/half-marathon clips.

> **F2** Reducer taxonomy: planetary (lower ratio, hands), harmonic (high ratio/precision, elbows/shoulders, weaker shock), RV (rigid, hips/knees/waist). TN curves govern torque vs speed; sim-reality mismatch → falls. Control loops sense joint state thousands of times/sec; IMU recovers balance after kicks. Tactile: piezoresistive, capacitive, piezoelectric, optical (hot) — ideally 3D pressure+shear for grasp.

> **F3** Industry structure: many parts shared with phones/EVs — why Honor/Xiaomi/Tesla/XPeng can field robots fast. Marathon chaos (falls, detached arms) vs Honor podium shows demo ≠ mass production. Guests: threshold 'yes and no' — assembling a dancer is easier than reliable leaf-catching manipulation + supply yield. Battery names overlap CATL/LG/EVE.

## Key Insights

> **1.** The flip era is a joint era.
>
> **Q** What changed in 1–2 years?
>
> **A** Motor+reducer actuators leapt in performance; hydraulics nostalgia ended for most humanoid demos.

> **2.** Shared supply chains lower entry, not mastery.
>
> **Q** Why can phone makers win marathons?
>
> **A** Parts and some stack algorithms already exist; winning reliability and dexterity is the scarce layer.

> **3.** Catching a leaf is the real benchmark.
>
> **Q** What replaces spectacle?
>
> **A** Contact-rich manipulation + yield at volume — hardware taxonomy exists; productization does not.
