## Key Facts

> **F1** Share and scale: China >70%. Official last-year attach ≈40 million cars; Yan reads that as ≈40% of global builds after a few years at 35% of revenue overseas. US about a third, Europe ≈20%. Group is adding 20–25% capacity toward 50 million vehicles — half a year of world production. That is the giant print. ROE stuck above 20% for 20-plus years. Revenue CAGR >10%, profit CAGR a point higher. 4% dividend plus a capex wave is the 'is there a ticket' question while the chart lagged the book in a parts recession.

> **F2** Margins versus peers: Fuyao ≈40% gross for 20 years and ≈20%+ net. Hesai opened 70–80% then sat at 40% for years. CATL opened mid-40s and slid to the 20s. Yan's first puzzle: a pane of glass holding 'tech' margins. Second: can you still buy the compounder. Third: the company is a teaching file — cycle timing, capital, and a management that did not follow every peer into a volume-for-share death.

> **F3** Industry: auto glass is not a commodity if you already tool the world's platforms. Ford-class US volume and Tesla-class EV glass (HUD, roof, weight) sit on the same line. Domestic EV price war smashed many parts P&Ls; Fuyao's print did not. Diligence the 20–25% capacity add: it is either the 50-million share grab or a cycle miss. Do not mark 3606.HK on last year's weak tape against a 20-year 40% gross. HUD glass is the mix upgrade on the tape: BMW work from 2015, now on cars in the low-¥100,000s, and a ~¥500-class HUD stack versus plain glass. Tesla is already on that line. Yan's Ford mailbox case is the US OEM book, not a glass multiple. The 4% dividend plus the 20–25% add is the ticket question while the chart lagged the 20-year file in a parts recession.
