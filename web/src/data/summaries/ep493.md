## Background

> First long-form with Noah Shinn on Instinct, the year-old personal assistant. Patrick wants what it already does (US Open tape, wardrobe shopping, cancelled subscriptions), the trusted-person network, why the product is free, and how a tiny company buys compute that doubles every week against Meta, Apple, and Amazon. Topics: take-rates, Muse, safety firewalls, and voice-first use.

---

## Key Facts

> **F1** Product and proof: Instinct is about a year old. No new app — it has a phone and a computer, so you text, call, or email it (Patrick has had about three calls). It fetched US Open jumbotron footage. Shoppers scan a wardrobe and get thousands of outfits rendered on their own body; some ask for three new looks a day. It cancels unused subscriptions and has come back with $2,000 this month saved. The trusted-person network is about 10 days old: two Instincts book a meeting; a six-person group had one Uber pick everyone up. Invite-only, five invites, started at 200 friends and is now 10–11% day-over-day with $0 of marketing. Invites sold on eBay for $300. Annualized volume is already over $1B on a tiny base; 50% is travel. Three weeks in, 40% have shared a card; one sensitive share prints about 80% retention.

> **F2** Money and incumbents: he will not run ads. Google, TikTok, Instagram, and Snapchat are the free-to-user, paid-to-influence model he refuses if the agent is smarter than the person. Volume is compounding at about 10% a day. He wants a merchant take-rate like Apple Pay: user free, merchant pays. A digital payment already has about 40 parties sharing 2–2.5%. Shopify takes about 2–3%, Amazon upwards of 10%, Apple in-app 30%. Boutique hotels will pay up to 30% for a booked room; half of volume is travel. Attention businesses get cut; ride or meal volume can rise if checkout is zero-friction — a car always waiting because Instinct has the calendar. More than 50% of traffic is not on iMessage.

> **F3** Compute and trust: the window versus the biggest players is months. He spends about 40% of his time on compute. 10% day-over-day doubles demand about every week; buy 2x and it is gone in a week, buy 10x and it is gone in a couple of weeks, and new clusters have a multi-month lead time (wrong by 3–4x if you miss). Even 5–8% for three to four months is 100 million users. They match Opus 5 engagement on cheaper batch shapes (3x to 8x more efficient). Goal is free for a lifetime, not a $100/month subscription. Early product lacked inbound firewalls and a decoupled action monitor; those now sit off the agent. Some users send more than 90% of messages by voice. Latest round is about $1B at roughly a $10B valuation, with Sequoia, Benchmark, and Coatue.

## Mental Model · *No App × Trust Ramp × Merchant Take-Rate*

> **Components**
>
> Meet people in text, call, and email, not a new icon. Capability is a computer plus a phone; the product job is understandability and feel, which is why word of mouth hit 10–11% day-over-day with five invites and no spend. Trust is a three-week ramp: 40% share a card, one sensitive share prints ≈80% retention. Keep the user in control of data. Money is a take-rate on merchants (Apple Pay shape), not ads that steer a smarter agent against the user. Compute is the constraint: demand doubles about weekly and clusters take months, so he buys ahead and customizes inference. Agents talking to agents (trusted network, calendar, Uber route) is the new social graph.
>
> **Application**
>
> Private:Instinct is the named vehicle: $1B-plus run-rate volume on a tiny invite base, ≈$10B raise, Sequoia/Benchmark/Coatue. AAPL is the take-rate map (Pay free to user; 30% in-app as the top of the curve) and one channel (iMessage), though more than half of traffic is already elsewhere. UBER is the zero-friction ride: if Instinct always has a car because it owns the calendar, trip volume of the underlying service should rise. AMZN sits on his 10% platform-fee mark and the Muse comparison — goods volume can go up, attention/upsell can get cut. Fade attention-only apps. Do not model this as a $100/month seat.

## Key Insights

>
> **Q** Is this still a demo?
>
> **A** Over $1B a year on a small invite base, half travel. Three-week card share is 40%. eBay was asking $300 for an invite.

> **2.** Charge the merchant, never the user.
>
> **Q** How do you stay aligned?
>
> **A** Ads would let a smarter agent sell against you. Apple Pay / Amex is the shape; Shopify 2–3%, Amazon ≈10%, Apple 30% are the curve.

> **3.** Compute doubles every week.
>
> **Q** What can kill the compounding?
>
> **A** Lead times of months at 5–8% day-over-day is 100 million users. He spends ≈40% of his time buying ahead. Wrong is 3–4x expensive.
