## Key Facts

> **F1** Scale and the broken five-year plan. One Piece is the best-selling manga and comic series Senra cites, over 600 million copies, with a Guinness record for copies of one series by a single author. Serialization began in Weekly Shonen Jump in July 1997. Oda expected the adventure to last about five years. In a 2022 interview he said he had badly underestimated it. The original plan now runs toward a 30th anniversary in 2027, and he has repeatedly underestimated how much story is left. He is 51 on this tape. Published wealth guesses run from $250 million to $500 million, with $20 million to $40 million a year from the work. For the 600 million copy mark he wrote the truth of the treasure and of Luffy, split the page, and sank the revealing half in a chest on the seabed, to be raised when the manga ends.

> **F2** The week and the sleep. A chapter has to move every seven days. Monday through Wednesday he writes story, dialogue, and rough boards. Thursday through Saturday he draws and finishes with assistants. Sunday is color, covers, and the rest. In a 2016 interview he described bed at 2:00 a.m. and wake at 5:00 a.m., held for roughly 15 years. An extra hour of sleep is an hour of work lost. Planning on four hours made him dread wasting that fourth hour. After a manuscript he allows one longer sleep. New ideas, he says, arrive only when he is tired and has skipped sleep and food, because that is when a person hits a limit. Assistants paint backgrounds. He draws people, animals, crowds, smoke, clouds, and the ocean himself, because another hand changes the moving line. He once stayed on the phone with an editor for 12 hours; the editor fell asleep. If the idea is not his, he does not want to draw it. They may criticize. They may not invent.

> **F3** One reader, then the guard dog. The audience in his head is himself at 15. He does not know how other people feel, so awesome means what that boy would love. An early editor said the women were not attractive. Oda could not sleep, practiced all night, and advanced through hand-drawn Disney — The Little Mermaid — frame by frame. On the Netflix live-action he refused a handoff. Netflix agreed not to release until he called it satisfactory. He read scripts, gave notes, and called himself a guard dog.
