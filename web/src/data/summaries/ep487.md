## Background

> First ILTB with Thompson since pre-ChatGPT aggregation-theory days. Patrick asks what 'the US wins AI' would even mean, then stress-tests compute shortages, Intel/Samsung as TSMC hedges, and whether data-center payback periods survive a memory-style bust.

---

## Key Facts

> **F1** Game theory opener: if US AI produces decisive military superiority, Thompson says China's optimal response is to destroy TSMC — so a clean American win is a dangerous world, not a desired one. Preferred status quo: OpenAI and Anthropic clearly frontier; Google opaque; Grok and Meta chasing; Chinese labs 'definitely distilling' to stay about 6–9 months back. He is skeptical China pulls fully ahead, but wants the stalemate to last.

> **F2** Funding: industry burned through FCF, then the debt markets in about a year; Google is issuing equity; NVIDIA is assembling a ≈$500B structure to tap pension funds and insurance float. Next step must be a return to free-cash-flow funding or a gap/blow-up. Even then 'AI is not going away.' Memory is the analog — Samsung took share by investing in downturns. Bulls say scarcity is forever; Thompson's worry is payback periods calculated in a shortage.

> **F3** Enterprise: Microsoft E7 at $100/user/month plus usage on top is a fraught bundle — the company sells 'everything a business needs' but usage pricing fights how CFOs think (loaded headcount as once-and-done, not a monthly meter). Apple will move some iPhone assembly to India only under duress and is not truly leaving China. Intel/Samsung logic get a second look as the TSMC dependency he has written about since 2013.

## Mental Model · *Stalemate Equilibrium × Capital-Curve Exhaustion × Scarcity Paybacks*

> **Components**
>
> Want a messy 6-to-9-month China lag via distillation, not a military US win that makes TSMC a target. Track the funding stack as it walks from free cash flow to debt to equity to NVIDIA's $500B pension/insurance tap. Treat data-center IRRs written in a memory-style shortage as fiction if abundance arrives. Read Microsoft E7 $100/user plus usage as the enterprise packaging fight, not a model-quality print. Apple-to-India is duress theater, not a China exit. Intel/Samsung are TSMC hedges Thompson has written since 2013, not a new thesis.
>
> **Application**
>
> Hold NVDA and TSM as the bottleneck pair of the equilibrium, not as 'America wins AI.' Fade incremental DC paper that only works while HBM and power stay tight. GOOGL equity issuance is a funding-stress tell. MSFT's meter fights how CFOs think about loaded headcount. A blow-up can still happen if FCF does not take the baton — models keep improving either way. Size China risk as TSMC kinetic, not as 'they cannot distill.'

## Key Insights

> **1.** A US AI win can be a war risk.
>
> **Q** What should we actually want?
>
> **A** A durable 6–9 month lead with distillation, not military monopoly that makes TSMC a target.

> **2.** The cheap money is already used up.
>
> **Q** Where does the next dollar come from?
>
> **A** After FCF, debt, and equity, NVIDIA's $500B pension tap is late-cycle. FCF has to return or something blows.

> **3.** Paybacks are being measured in a shortage.
>
> **Q** What rhymes with memory?
>
> **A** Samsung invested into busts. If abundance arrives, today's DC IRRs were fiction.
