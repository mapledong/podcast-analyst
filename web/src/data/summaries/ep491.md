## Key Facts

> **F1** Family and method: Moritz grew up a Jewish-refugee minority in 1960s–70s Wales. Parents left Germany in the 1930s and refused the word survivor — they were never in the camps. Paternal grandfather, a Bavarian judge and Verdun Iron Cross, lost the post in 1933, clung to a pension, and was shipped east. Maternal grandfather, a nearly blind cattle trader, left with 72 hours. Residue: extra passports. First-14-years interviews began at Time on Lee Iacocca, always the outsider under Henry Ford II. Anna, the Catholic nanny, is righteous at Yad Vashem. A French-village series sorted resistors, the silent, and collaborators; COVID Zooms found resistors rare. Bill Deeds told a 22-year-old to go to America; Time hired him with no clips. The Computer-of-the-Year issue — Jobs sure he was Man of the Year — never healed. Intensity fades after the early 30s; the inner outsider does not.

> **F2** Sequoia: Don Valentine hired him around 1985 when the firm fit around Patrick's table. Five letters went out; the others said no engineering degree, try PR. Valentine pointed at Tommy Davis and Arthur Rock (Fairchild, Teledyne with Singleton, Intel day one, Apple). Mid-80s venture, he says, a bright 14-to-16-year-old could have done. He took the 1995 firm and stepped down in 2012–13: Israel, China, then Heritage with Kevin and Keith. One broken fund was fought back to par over about 10 years because non-profit LPs should not pay for an index. He ghost-wrote Alex Ferguson (1986–2013, 26 seasons). Early X.com backer. Xiaomi started as a 2010 phone maker and now builds cars he says beat Porsches. Siebel sold for $10B when that was a lot of money; Facebook and Google IPOs were when $100B was a lot; a six-year-old AI company can be worth trillions. Trump common vocabulary is about 2,400 words versus Obama at about 4,500.

> **F3** After the chair: he left cold turkey once he was sick. Harriet and he started a foundation about 25 years ago; with Griff Gaffney he launched the San Francisco Standard as a for-profit, center-right local paper with no thumb on news. He still advises Heritage. Two sons in their 30s, grandchildren. His mother, who died weeks before COVID, was sure the police should come for him after the Yahoo IPO. Next book idea is 1940s Iran as fiction mixed with nonfiction. The Stripe memo on what could go right was famously short. Kindest thing anyone did: marry him. Parenting residue: arm children with confidence and freedom. Antisemitism, he says, has 2,500 years of history and will not vanish in 2026 or 2030.

## Mental Model · *First-14 × Extra Passports × Great Person*

> **Components**
>
> Paint a founder from marks on a canvas, most of them before age 14. Refugee households teach survival, suspicion, and a bolt-hole: never enough passports. Resistors are rare; most people stay silent, which is how bad years happen. Monomania builds companies and ruins families (Evelyn Waugh's pram in the hallway; Frank Auerbach's one day off a year). Venture in the mid-80s had imperfect information and few intermediaries; that edge is gone. The firm you already have can be the best investment if you refuse to rest on old wins and if non-profit LPs get a real attempt at par. Institutions endure, but he still bets the great person — Jobs, Iacocca, Ferguson, Elon.
>
> **Application**
>
> Private:Sequoia is the named favorite: 1985 hire, 1995-to-2012/13 run, Israel/China/Heritage, one fund clawed to par. AAPL is the Jobs-and-Rock line from this tape, not a new iPhone print — Time's computer-of-the-year issue is why the friendship never reset. TSLA is the Elon print: early X.com, greatest industrialist in the history of America, and the claim that tools now let rare people run more than one company (Xiaomi 2010 phones to Porsche-beating cars). Do not copy 1985 information edges. Fade awe; keep extra optionality.
