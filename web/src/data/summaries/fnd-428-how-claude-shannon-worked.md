## Key Facts

> **F1** 1948 paper introduces the bit and is widely called one of the 20th century's most influential scientific papers — theoretical foundation for digital communications, the internet, compression, and error correction. Senra tells friends Shannon is 'the reason Anthropic can exist.' After the paper he becomes a scientific celebrity: Bell Labs sinecure and MIT 'do whatever you want' offers; Shannon is to communications as Einstein is to physics — and he does not care.

> **F2** Method: a genius is 'someone who is usefully irritated.' Shannon: he gets a kick from a circuit that uses very little equipment for great results. Six strategies for problem-solving; productivity hack is leaving the pad for jazz, clarinet, juggling, unicycle. He never argued ideas — if people did not believe them, he ignored those people. Principle of indifference: chase instincts at the expense of prestigious options (the information-theory paper itself).

> **F3** Life texture: gadgets from unicycles to chess-playing robots; tea with Turing, meetings with von Neumann and Einstein; later obsession with stocks as 'just another puzzle' that made him rich. Henry Singleton cameos as the conglomerate builder Buffett/Munger actually copied. Senra's point for operators: play and abstraction beat status-seeking after the breakthrough.

## Mental Model · *Play × Abstraction × Indifference to Status*

> **Components**
>
> Treat unlike problems as one information problem. Protect unstructured play (unicycle hours) as the research method. Refuse to debate unbelievers. Take the blank-check chair (MIT) only if it buys more instinct-chasing, not a pundit career.
>
> **Application**
>
> R&D orgs: measure cleverness of the circuit, not slide-count. After a landmark paper, decline the speaking circuit. Puzzle-hungry capital allocation (Shannon's stocks) is a personality, not a process you can copy from a documentary.
