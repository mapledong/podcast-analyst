## Key Facts

> **F1** Marks: AfterQuery $300 million in April, $3.2 billion in September. Scale >$29 billion on the Meta minority print. Mercor $10 billion (Oct last year). Handshake-class recruiting shops, old crowdsource Scale, new synthetic shops, and embodied-data defectors are He's taxonomy. The product moved from tagging pictures and scoring answers to hiring domain experts to write rubrics — what 'good' looks like — and to build RL environments with tools and feedback so agents can fail in software. The tape is dated 2026; treat every cap and percent as of the recording, not as a 2027 model.

> **F2** Exams: vertical finance/law boards plus whole-workflow agent tests. ALE is the named academic attempt — 150+ public tasks now, 50+ industries, v2 targeting 1,000+ in months. He says tech-report numbers have randomness; some boards people use never appear in the blog post. Sun: the real distribution is user experience. LMArena worked when a reply took seconds. A multi-hour agent job cannot be A/B'd by a tired user opening four agents.

> **F3** Why labs pay: post-training and eval are now the bottleneck, not more raw web scrape. Scale sells data plus methodology. Newcomers sell speed and a narrower expert network. Embodied/robotics data is called out as a separate gene. None of the $29 billion or $3.2 billion prints come with a margin bridge on this tape — treat them as scarcity marks on expert-hours and exam design.

## Mental Model · *Expert Hours × Rubric/RL Env × Exam Capture*

> **Components**
>
> Labs do not need more JPEGs. They need experts to write rubrics and environments where an agent can act. Scale is the old crowdsource gene at a $29 billion Meta-marked cap. AfterQuery is the YC speed gene at $3.2 billion. Mercor is the recruiting gene at $10 billion. ALE tries to make the exam public and wide (150 → 1,000 tasks). Hour-long agent jobs break LMArena-style voting. Internal lab A/Bs are the hidden ground truth. Keep the method weekly: name the bottleneck (bandwidth, exam hours, token bill, trust, weather window) and only then pick the ticker. Numbers on this tape beat category slides. If the guest cannot price the unit — tokens, tasks, hectares, or a 40-yuan checkout — the mark is a story, not a position.
>
> **Application**
>
> META is the Scale cap-table print, not a data-vendor operating thesis. AfterQuery and Mercor are private scarcity marks — diligence expert supply and whether the exam is real work or leaderboard farming. Do not confuse a 10x in six months with a moat. Expert hours do not scale like GPU hours. A 10x mark in six months is a scarcity print on people who can write a rubric, not a software multiple. ALE's jump from 150 to 1,000 tasks is the public-exam attempt to make that scarcity visible.

## Key Insights

> **1.** The SKU is the exam, not the label.
>
> **Q** What are labs buying?
>
> **A** Rubrics, RL environments, and long-horizon tasks. Image tagging is the old business.

> **2.** AfterQuery is the speed mark.
>
> **Q** What did six months do?
>
> **A** $300 million to $3.2 billion. Fastest YC-to-unicorn on the tape. Not a margin story.

> **3.** Leaderboards lag agent time.
>
> **Q** Why don't scores feel real?

> **A** LMArena needs a seconds-long reply. ALE's hour-long jobs cannot be voted the same way.
