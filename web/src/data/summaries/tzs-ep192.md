## Key Facts

> **F1** Three gates: analyze business model, bargaining power, and sources of advantage (Competition Demystified — not every firm can be DCF'd); only then estimate intrinsic value vs price, with required discount shrinking as confidence rises; all under circle-of-competence limits.

> **F2** HK vs A-share: stronger dividend culture and earlier adaptation to slower growth; huge amplitude; foreign flows (e.g. underweight HK / overweight India) drain liquidity. Do not casually: non-Stock-Connect names, or 0.2× PB + cash piles that 'must' re-rate.

> **F3** Compounding/risk/patience: pros differ from amateurs mainly by exhausting industry work; methodology matches legendary retail deep-workers, scale/risk budget differ. Patience means laying out early on economically sound but unpopular names — not inspiration-driven 5% clips.

## Key Insights

> **1.** Not every company can be valued with a DCF.
>
> **Q** What is step one in stock picking?
>
> **A** Be a business analyst first: moat, pricing power, commercial fit. Only after that gate do long-term growth and margin assumptions matter — otherwise the model is a number game.

> **2.** HK has dividend culture — and distinctive traps.
>
> **Q** What should A-share listeners watch?
>
> **A** Willingness to pay real cash dividends is a maturity feature; but non-Connect names, chronic cheapness hoping for re-rating, and false confidence amid huge swings are the 'do not try casually' list.

> **3.** Edge is cognition plus patience, not inspiration.
>
> **Q** Where does compounding come from?
>
> **A** Spend the hours to know an industry, then be willing to sit left of popularity. A rigorous research SOP tracks known names and external idea sources — not a five-point buy after an AI chat.
