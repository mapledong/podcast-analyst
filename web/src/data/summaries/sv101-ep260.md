## Key Facts

> **F1** The week the tape is about. Recorded November 17, earnings night. The board was still insisting Huang would not be asked back. The open the show publishes is November 23: Nvidia reappoints him, he says he made a serious mistake and is returning, the stock opens about 30% higher, and the neo-clouds tied to Nvidia, CoreWeave, Nebius, and IREN, rise more than 20%. Maggie reads the print as demand that is not in doubt. Agent workflows show up as data-center orders for hyperscalers and neo-clouds. The interim CEO, who had been the CFO, also said vendor financing would be reviewed more carefully, and that guarantees begin when a project can be put into service, not while it is stuck. That is the line the market still has to digest. A week of Nvidia headlines moved on the order of hundreds of billions across the AI complex.

> **F2** Two strategies inside one guarantee. Ethan and Maggie both treat Nvidia as the company whose words move the build, maybe the single most important firm in the AI industry. A month or two earlier, high interest on AI borrowers had already hit the stocks. Huang told the market the operators were fine and that Nvidia did not even need to step in. Confidence in the neo-clouds returned. The worry on November 17 is that Huang and Colette Kress had disagreed on guarantees and financing for these clouds. If he is not CEO, implicit promises may not be kept when a partner's cash is about to break. The question is whether Nvidia still supplies emergency money and a guarantee in a crunch, for the industry rather than for one credit. They say that has to be watched, not assumed from the old habit.

> **F3** Who borrows, and who eats a delay. The show's earlier video was on how large clouds use capital markets and leverage for capex. This hour goes from debt and power, with Nvidia as the start. CoreWeave's stock is the roller coaster of the year, including a move of more than 20% on November 23. Microsoft, Meta, and CoreWeave are all building data centers and do not borrow at the same cost or on the same conditions. If a project slips, someone has to carry the financial risk. The guarantee that starts only when the site can be turned on leaves the construction period with the borrower. Power is Ethan's half: planning a data center is an energy-transition problem, not only a chip order. Demand is the part Maggie calls affirmed. The credit terms are the part that still depends on who is CEO and how far the CFO will sign.

Huang and Colette Kress split on how far neo-cloud guarantees go. A guarantee that starts at switch-on leaves the delay with the borrower. Microsoft, Meta, and CoreWeave do not pay the same rate. Agent demand is not the questioned half. Hundreds of billions move on his sentences.

> **Application**

## Key Insights

> **1.** The open and the taping are six days apart.
>
> **Q** What changed by November 23?
>
> **A** Taped November 17, board still refusing a return. The published open is Huang back as CEO, the stock up about 30%, and CoreWeave, Nebius, and IREN up more than 20%.

> **2.** Demand is not the argument. The signature is.
>
> **Q** Where did Huang and the CFO split?
>
> **A** On guarantees and financing for neo-clouds. The print says support continues but vendor financing gets a harder look, and the guarantee starts when the project can be turned on.

> **3.** Three builders, three borrowing costs.
>
> **Q** Who eats a delay?
>
> **A** Microsoft, Meta, and CoreWeave do not borrow on the same terms. CoreWeave is the year's roller coaster. A late site sits with the borrower until the guarantee begins.
